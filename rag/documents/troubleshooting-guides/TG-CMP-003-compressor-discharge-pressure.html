Centrifugal Compressor Troubleshooting Guide - Discharge Pressure and Temperature

1. Scope

This guide supports troubleshooting of recurring high discharge pressure and high discharge temperature alarms on EastRefinery centrifugal compressors, including Recycle Gas Compressor K-301 (HCU-2) and Wet Gas Compressor K-302 (CDU-1).

2. Why Discharge Pressure Alarms Recur

Repeated high discharge pressure alarms are rarely caused by the compressor itself. The most common contributing factors are:

  • Degraded discharge cooler performance. A high outlet temperature on the discharge cooler (E-301 for K-301) raises downstream pressure drop and pushes the operating point towards the alarm. Fouled air-cooler fin banks, a failed cooler fan or closed louvres are typical causes, and the effect is strongest on hot afternoons.
  • Anti-surge valve deviation. An anti-surge valve (FV-301) that does not follow controller output changes the recycle flow and therefore the discharge pressure.
  • Downstream restriction. A partially closed downstream block valve or high reactor loop pressure.
  • Gas composition change. Higher molecular weight increases the developed head.

3. Diagnostic Steps

  1. Trend discharge pressure together with discharge cooler outlet temperature for the last 24 hours. A cooler outlet temperature rise that precedes the pressure alarm by 5 to 15 minutes confirms the cooler as the contributing factor.
  2. Compare anti-surge valve position feedback with controller output. A deviation greater than 5 % requires an instrument technician to check the positioner.
  3. Check cooler fan motors are running and fin banks are clean. Schedule fin-bank washing if the approach temperature is more than 8 degC above design.
  4. Confirm downstream block valves are fully open.

4. Immediate Operator Actions

Reduce compressor load within the approved operating envelope to keep discharge pressure below the trip setting of 175 bar(g). Keep the anti-surge controller in automatic. Do not open the anti-surge valve manually to relieve pressure unless instructed by the shift supervisor, because manual recycle can overheat the compressor suction.

5. Surge

A Surge Detected alarm requires immediate confirmation that the anti-surge controller is in automatic and the valve is responding. Repeated surge events require a compressor performance review before the machine is returned to normal load.
